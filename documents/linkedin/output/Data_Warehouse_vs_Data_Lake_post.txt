A data lake is a scalable raw‑data repository; a data warehouse is a curated, analytics‑ready store. The choice hinges on preservation needs, consumer profiles, and workload types.

𝗛𝗼𝘄 𝗶𝘁 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸𝘀 𝗶𝗻 𝗽𝗿𝗮𝗰𝘁𝗶𝗰𝗲

Sources → Raw Data → Processing → Analytics / ML
Sources → Transform → Warehouse → BI / Analytics

An e‑commerce platform pulls PostgreSQL orders, Salesforce customers, payment API transactions, Kafka clickstream, and JSON logs.

• The lake keeps all source files intact for future re‑processing or ML experiments.
• The warehouse turns them into fact_orders, dim_customer, dim_product, and derived metrics like daily_sales and customer_LTV for business reporting.

𝗞𝗲𝘆 𝘁𝗿𝗮𝗱𝗲‑𝗼𝗳𝗳𝘀

• Lake

◦ Flexible, supports CSV, JSON, Parquet, logs, images, and any schema applied later.
◦ Handles large‑scale processing and ML pipelines.
◦ Requires robust governance, metadata, and data‑quality controls to avoid becoming a "data swamp."

• Warehouse

◦ Curated, SQL‑centric, ready for BI tools and quick queries.
◦ Strong modeling and governance reduce data‑consistency risks.
◦ Needs upfront design; may not accommodate every raw data type as a primary home.

𝗥𝘂𝗹𝗲 𝗼𝗳 𝘁𝗵𝘂𝗺𝗯

Ask: "What data must we preserve, who will consume it, and what workloads must it support?"
The answer often is 𝗯𝗼𝘁𝗵-a lake for raw, exploratory work and a warehouse for business‑ready insights, or a lakehouse that blends the two.
