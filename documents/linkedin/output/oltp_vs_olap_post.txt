Heavy analytical queries on a live order‑processing database is a silent performance killer.
When a customer places an order, the OLTP system must keep up.
Running month‑over‑month revenue reports on the same engine can make both workloads lag.

• OLTP (Online Transaction Processing)
  ◦ Frequent, short, concurrent transactions
  ◦ INSERT / UPDATE / DELETE operations
  ◦ Low‑latency reads and writes
  ◦ Highly normalized schemas
  ◦ Strong consistency and transactional guarantees

• OLAP (Online Analytical Processing)
  ◦ Large scans and aggregations
  ◦ Complex joins
  ◦ Historical data
  ◦ Often denormalized or star schemas
  ◦ Read‑heavy workloads

Imagine an e‑commerce platform:

• Operational flow – Customer → Cart → Payment → Order → Inventory
• Analytical question – “What was monthly revenue by region and product category over the last 3 years?”

Trade‑offs:

• Heavy analytical scans compete with transactional CPU, memory, and I/O, degrading latency.
• Using an OLAP warehouse for thousands of tiny transactional updates is inefficient and defeats its read‑optimised design.

Rule of thumb: isolate OLTP and OLAP.
Use CDC/ETL/ELT to move changes into a dedicated warehouse, then feed BI tools.
