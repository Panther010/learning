OLTP vs OLAP they both store data, but they solve very different problems.
OLTP → Run the business
OLAP → Understand the business

OLTP (Online Transaction Processing) is designed for frequent, short, concurrent transactions:
INSERT / UPDATE / DELETE
Low-latency reads and writes
Highly normalized schemas
Strong consistency and transactional guarantees

OLAP (Online Analytical Processing) is designed for analytical workloads:
Large scans and aggregations
Complex joins
Historical data
Often denormalized/star schemas
Optimized for read-heavy workloads

Real-world example
Imagine an e-commerce platform.
When a customer places an order:
Customer → Cart → Payment → Order → Inventory
That operational workflow belongs in OLTP.

But when the business asks:
"What was monthly revenue by region and product category over the last 3 years?"
That's an OLAP workload.

A typical architecture might look like:
OLTP DB → CDC/ETL/ELT → Data Warehouse → BI/Analytics

Trade-off
Trying to run heavy analytical queries directly on an OLTP database can compete with production transactions for CPU, memory and I/O.
Conversely, using an OLAP warehouse to handle thousands of tiny transactional updates isn't what it is optimized for.
That's why modern data architectures usually separate the two workloads.

Takeaway
Think of it this way:

OLTP = What is happening right now?
OLAP = What has happened, and what can we learn from it?

The real engineering challenge isn't choosing one over the other.
It's designing the data flow between them.
