Batch vs Micro-batch vs Streaming:
How fast does your data actually need to move?

The right processing model depends on how quickly the business needs the data and how much complexity it is willing to operate.

Think about the three models like this:

🐢 Batch
Collect data → Process → Publish

    Orders
      ↓
    Hourly/Daily batch
      ↓
    Transform
      ↓
    Warehouse

Best for:
    → Daily reporting
    → Large historical transformations
    → Backfills
    → Periodic ETL

Simple, predictable and efficient — but higher latency.

⚡ Micro-batch
Collect a small window → Process → Publish

    Events
      ↓
    30-second window
      ↓
    Transform
      ↓
    Dashboard

Best for:
    → Near-real-time dashboards
    → Incremental loads
    → Clickstream aggregation
    → Monitoring

A useful middle ground when seconds/minutes of latency are acceptable.
And an important nuance: Micro-batch is often an implementation approach for streaming workloads rather than a completely separate category.

🚀 Streaming
Event arrives → Process → Output

    Payment
      ↓
    Validate
      ↓
    Fraud rules
      ↓
    Alert

Best for:
    → Fraud detection
    → Real-time alerts
    → IoT
    → Recommendations
    → Operational event processing

But streaming isn't just "faster batch."
Now you have to think about:
    → Event ordering
    → Late events
    → Duplicates
    → State
    → Checkpointing
    → Recovery
    → Delivery semantics

The operational complexity goes up significantly.
The interesting part: the same company can use all three.
An e-commerce platform could use:

Batch: Daily revenue reporting
Micro-batch: Sales dashboard refreshed every 30 seconds
Streaming: Fraud detection for incoming payments

There is no rule saying your entire platform must be batch or streaming.
My rule of thumb:
Hours/days → Batch
Seconds/minutes → Micro-batch
Milliseconds/seconds + immediate action → Streaming

But don't stop at latency.
Ask:
What's the cost of being late?
A monthly report being 30 minutes late may not matter.
A fraud decision being 30 minutes late is probably useless.
And if the business doesn't need real-time data, don't introduce real-time complexity just because the technology allows it.
Optimize for the lowest latency the business actually needs — not the lowest latency you can build.
